# Branched isothermal pipe pressure waves

`h2physics.isothermal_pipe_network_waves` advances a connected graph of finite-volume pipe segments with a shared `IsothermalSinglePhaseEOS`. Construct explicit `IsothermalPipeNetworkNode` and `IsothermalPipeNetworkEdge` records, then call `IsothermalPipeNetworkWaveModel.simulate()` with one initial pressure and velocity array per edge. Every node and edge carries owner/evidence identifiers and an explicit origin; the model and output retain the caller clock, EOS identity and fixed temperature.

## Equations and junction closure

For each pipe, the solver advances the one-dimensional isothermal Euler mass and momentum balances with a first-order Rusanov flux and optional constant Darcy momentum drag. It checks the p-T/rho-T EOS round-trip and uses the shared isothermal characteristic speed; there is no silent ideal-gas fallback. All edges use one explicit EOS and fixed temperature. Gravity/elevation changes, heat transfer, composition changes and phase transitions are rejected or outside the model.

At each internal junction, pressure is common across incident edge traces. For each port, the interior outgoing characteristic is `w_- = v - integral(c_T/rho d rho)`, where velocity is measured outward from the node. Holding this invariant between the adjacent cell and the common-pressure EOS density gives `v_node = v_cell + integral(c_T/rho d rho)`. In the linearized limit, `delta v ≈ delta p/(rho*c_T)`, so a node pressure above the adjacent pipe pressure drives outward flow. The node pressure is solved inside caller-supplied positive bounds so the sum of the *same outward Rusanov mass fluxes used by the finite-volume edge updates* is zero. The sampled continuity residual must be monotonic with exactly one bracketed root before bisection; absent or ambiguous roots, EOS-range failures, and supersonic adjacent/trace states are rejected. Closed terminal nodes apply zero mass flux and pressure traction.

Because each junction's solved flux is exactly the boundary mass flux inserted into the connected edge updates, junction transfers cancel in the global network mass ledger. Trace outputs include edge cell pressure/velocity, edge face mass flow, junction pressure and continuity residual, total mass and global residual, plus caller geometry/state provenance.

## Deferred case and limitations

The five independently schedulable synthetic Hangang contracts use a three-branch junction and closed terminal walls: `hydrogen-pipeline-branched-isothermal-wave-network` checks basic node/edge state, provenance and mass closure; `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-reversed-orientation-order` checks edge orientation and node/edge insertion-order invariance; `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-unequal-branch-areas` checks area-weighted junction mass balance; `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-darcy-friction` exercises nonzero Darcy friction against a zero-friction receipt; and `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-pressure-bound-failure` checks rejection when no junction root lies inside caller bounds. They are deferred and are not run evidence.

The junction law is an idealized common-pressure/mass-conservation condition, not a detailed tee loss, mixing chamber, valve, compressor, or finite-volume junction cell. The network must be connected, horizontal, single-phase, fixed-temperature, and use explicit caller pressure bounds at each junction. Junction roots must be uniquely bracketed; this model does not invent states when the declared bracket cannot support a subsonic solution. The scheme is first order, uses a global conservative CFL step, and has no transient measurement qualification.

## Prescribed-pressure reservoir terminal nodes

A degree-one node may be declared `kind="reservoir"` and supplied through `simulate(..., reservoir_boundaries={node_id: FiniteReservoirPressureBoundary(...)})`. The existing boundary record supplies pressure knots, interpolation clock, EOS identity, exact fixed temperature, state owner/evidence, and origin. Its support must cover the complete simulation interval; all pressure knots are replayed through the network EOS, and intermediate interpolated states are checked again at each substep. The pipe-adjacent and boundary characteristic states must remain subsonic. Missing/extra nodes, identity mismatch, unsupported EOS state, or inadequate time coverage reject.

The output retains sampled prescribed pressure and boundary identity/owner/evidence/clock/origin. Signed outward Rusanov mass flow at prescribed-pressure reservoir nodes is integrated into cumulative pipe-side inflow and outflow; the global residual is `M_pipe - M_pipe,0 - m_pipe,in + m_pipe,out`. These ledgers close the pipe network only. They do not estimate the source reservoir's inventory or depletion; pressure history remains caller-owned. The two deferred contracts are `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-prescribed-reservoir-history` and `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-reservoir-contract-rejections`.

## Finite isothermal inventory terminals

A distinct terminal `kind="finite_reservoir"` accepts a `FiniteIsothermalPipeReservoir` in `simulate(..., finite_reservoirs={node_id: provider})`. The provider identifies its exact node, state owner/evidence, clock, EOS, temperature, fixed volume, initial mass, admissible mass interval and origin. Every step obtains its boundary pressure from the current reservoir mass through the shared EOS and checks the inverse density relation. Clock, EOS and temperature must match the network exactly, as must node and state-owner/evidence IDs.

At each internal step, the solver uses the terminal Rusanov outward mass rate both in the pipe's finite-volume face and in `m_next = m_current - dt*F_out`. Positive `F_out` transfers inventory into the pipe; negative `F_out` fills the reservoir. Depletion, an exceeded inventory bound, EOS domain failure, and sonic/supersonic terminal flow reject the step. No clipping or alternative valve flow is inferred. The trace stores separate endpoint mass and pressure arrays and provider provenance. `total_mass_kg` now represents pipe plus finite-reservoir inventory when this model is present; its residual closes against prescribed-pressure terminal exchange only. Thus transfers between the finite inventory and pipe cancel internally, while any separate prescribed-pressure boundaries remain external pipe-side exchanges.

This model is distinct from `FiniteReservoirPressureBoundary`, which is a prescribed pressure-versus-time input with no source inventory/depletion state. Both interfaces can be used in one network, but the two types of boundary ledger remain separate. The two deferred finite-inventory contracts are `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-finite-inventory-reservoir` and `hydrogen-pipeline-network-wave-finite-inventory-rejections`; the state-level suite also includes `hydrogen-pipeline-finite-isothermal-reservoir-eos-and-mass-step` and `hydrogen-pipeline-finite-isothermal-reservoir-rejection-contracts`.

The reservoir is an idealized fixed-volume, fixed-temperature, well-mixed, single-phase inventory. No energy balance, heat transfer, vessel wall, regulator, valve/orifice, mixing volume, or vessel structural model is included. The direct endpoint-flux coupling is not independent validation of a physical storage vessel. See [`FINITE_ISOTHERMAL_PIPE_RESERVOIR.md`](FINITE_ISOTHERMAL_PIPE_RESERVOIR.md) for equations, ownership and limits.
